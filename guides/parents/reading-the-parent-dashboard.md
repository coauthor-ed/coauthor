The parent area shows how your child has been learning. This guide explains what each part tells you, and where to be careful.

## Overview

A short summary of the last few days: time spent learning, lessons touched, recent test results, and a few **things to notice**. Read this first. It is written to start a conversation, not to judge.

![The Overview tab of the parent area: one card for the child with lessons finished, stars, a 4 day streak and minutes spent, and a list of recent lessons and tests.](media/parent-overview.png "The Overview tab. The numbers here are example data.")

## Progress

- **Lessons** - which lessons are started, in progress or finished. "Finished" means your child reached the last slide.
- **Tests** - the latest results first, with the score for each finished test.
- **XP each day** - a bar for each day. Every lesson step and test answer earns some XP.

![The Progress tab: test results, a bar for the XP earned on each of the last seven days, and the lessons started and finished.](media/parent-progress.png "The Progress tab, with example data.")

> [!TIP]
> XP measures practice, not understanding. A day with lots of XP and many wrong answers can be a very good day: your child was trying, guessing first, and learning from mistakes. That is how the lessons are meant to be used.

## What the numbers do not tell you

- **A wrong answer in a lesson is not a failure.** Lessons ask children to guess before they are taught. Wrong guesses are expected and cost nothing.
- **A test score is one sitting.** Look at the questions behind it (in the Practice section) before drawing conclusions.
- **Time spent is not effort.** A tab left open counts as time too.

## Practice tests

In **Practice**, you can build a test for your child from a course's question bank: choose the course, the chapters and how many questions. The test then appears on your child's home page. Results show here once it is finished.

## Talking about it

Some questions that work better than "Why did you get this wrong?":

- "What surprised you in this lesson?"
- "Which guess was furthest off? What do you think now?"
- "Can you explain this one to me?"

Explaining an idea to someone else is one of the best ways to learn it.
